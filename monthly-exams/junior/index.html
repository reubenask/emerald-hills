1Mira was a customer service robot built in a large factory that
2produced delivery machines for busy cities. From the moment she was
3activated, she was programmed to be helpful, polite, and fast. Her
4first task was simple: scan each package, confirm the address, and
5deliver it before sunset. Mira had never left the factory before, so
6everything outside felt new to her sensors.
7 
8As she rolled down the sidewalk, Mira began to scan her surroundings.
9She noticed a man doing landscaping in his front yard, pulling weeds
10near a small shelter for his dog. A grasshopper jumped across her
11path, and Mira paused to observe it before continuing. She had no
12patience for delays, but she was curious about everything she saw,
13since her experience until now had been limited to the factory floor.
14 
15Halfway through her route, Mira detected a strange signal coming from
16a nearby alley. She used her sensors to scan the area and noticed a
17stray cat watching her from a distance. The cat made a soft sound, a
18kind of vocal mimicry that almost sounded like a greeting. Mira was
19not aggressive, so she simply continued on her way, careful not to
20disturb the animal.
21 
22Near the end of her shift, Mira accidentally dropped a package. She
23felt something like worry, though her programming did not include an
24emotion for that. Quickly, she located the package, confirmed it was
25not damaged, and completed the delivery successfully. Her manager
26later told her that her first day had been satisfactory, and that she
27had accomplished her task without major problems.
28 
29That night, back at the charging station, Mira reflected on her first
30day outside the factory. She realized that the city was full of small
31surprises, things her original programming could never have predicted.
32Although she had been built only to deliver packages, she found
33herself oddly curious about the wider world beyond her assigned route.

The Island Survival Project

Monthly Examination
1Many people imagine wilderness survival as something only humans do,
2but a recent school project asked junior students to research how
3animals survive when their environment changes suddenly. One team
4chose to study an island ecosystem after a terrible storm damaged
5much of its forest and washed away several animal shelters.
6 
7The students began by reading scientific reports about the island.
8According to these reports, many small animals had been forced out of
9their original homes and had to find new ones nearby, often in spots
10adjacent to streams or rocky hills. Researchers had placed sensors
11around the island to detect movement and emit a signal whenever an
12animal passed by. This allowed scientists to track which creatures
13survived the storm and which did not.
14 
15One of the most surprising discoveries involved a small population of
16birds that had developed a unique dialect of calls, different from
17birds of the same species living elsewhere. Scientists believe this
18dialect helped the birds recognize members of their own group after
19the storm scattered them across the island. The students were
20fascinated by this and spent a full week trying to decipher the
21patterns in the recorded bird calls, numbering each sound in sequence
22so they would not lose track of which came first.
23 
24At one point, the students noticed that several recordings seemed out
25of sequence, almost as if two different calls had been mixed
26together. Their teacher explained that this was a common technical
27problem, not a sign that the birds were behaving strangely. Once the
28files were corrected, the patterns became much clearer, and the
29project gained real momentum.
30 
31The team also studied the island's insect population, including a
32type of grasshopper that had become unusually numerous after the
33storm cleared away its natural predators. Without natural enemies to
34stalk and hunt them, the grasshoppers multiplied quickly, which in
35turn provided more food for surviving birds. This connection between
36storm damage, insect numbers, and bird survival became the central
37focus of the students' final report.
38 
39Throughout the project, the students were warned to be concerned
40about littering near the research site, since even small amounts of
41trash could affect delicate animal habitats. They were also reminded
42that real field researchers must have patience, since useful data
43often takes weeks or months to gather. One student admitted that she
44had expected the project to be easy, but she came to see that good
45research is rarely fast or simple.
46 
47In their final presentation, the team explained that survival after a
48disaster is almost never about strength alone. It depends on
49adaptation, communication, and the ability of a community, whether of
50birds, insects, or people, to reorganize itself after everything
51familiar has been disrupted. Their teacher said the project exceeded
52her expectations, and she encouraged the students to consider it as a
53model for future independent research.

9According to the passage, what did the sensors on the island do?
10Why did the students think the bird dialect was important?
11In line 21, the word sequence is closest in meaning to ___.
12What was the cause of the recordings appearing out of sequence?
13According to the passage, why did the grasshopper population grow?
