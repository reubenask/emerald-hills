Part 3 - Read an Academic Passage

Questions 11-20

Managing Rainwater in Cities

For many years, flood control in cities focused on moving rainwater away as quickly as possible. Engineers built concrete channels and underground pipes to carry runoff into rivers. These systems can work well during ordinary storms, but extremely heavy rainfall may exceed their capacity. When too much water arrives at once, streets and basements can flood.

Some cities are therefore supplementing traditional drainage with green infrastructure. Rain gardens, planted with species that tolerate both wet and dry conditions, collect water from nearby roofs and roads. Their soil allows some water to soak into the ground, while plants use part of it. Permeable paving works in a related way: small spaces in the surface allow rain to pass through rather than run immediately into a drain.

These methods have benefits beyond flood management. Vegetation can cool nearby areas and provide habitats for insects. By filtering some pollutants, rain gardens may also improve water quality. However, their performance depends on local conditions. Compacted soil absorbs little water, and poorly maintained gardens may become clogged. It is therefore inaccurate to assume that simply adding plants will solve every drainage problem.

Planning matters as much as design. A small garden in the wrong place may collect little runoff, whereas several gardens along a suitable route can reduce the volume reaching a drain. Officials need information about slopes, soil, and existing pipes before choosing locations. Residents can help with maintenance, but relying entirely on unpaid volunteers may be unrealistic in neighborhoods where people have limited spare time.

Green infrastructure is best understood as one element of a broader strategy. Cities still need conventional drainage, careful maintenance, and plans for exceptional storms. Combining these approaches can distribute the work of managing rainwater instead of expecting a single system to handle every situation.
