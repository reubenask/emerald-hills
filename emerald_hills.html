Lesson Structure

What a normal lesson looks like

Every lesson follows a six-step cycle. Students encounter language through experience first — never through memorisation from a list. The topic stays consistent across every step, binding vocabulary, meaning, and skills together.

01

Watch

A documentary, film clip, TV series segment, debate, or news report on a chosen topic. Students encounter language naturally through context before seeing a written word.

02

Class discussion

Students discuss plot, ideas, characters, and reactions. Unfamiliar words are identified and explored in context — what they heard, where they appeared, how they were used in sentences.

03

Derive vocabulary

Meaning is inferred from context and visuals, never memorised from a list. Students learn to decode language the way fluent speakers do — through exposure and reasoning.

04

Read a passage

The same topic becomes a reading passage. Vocabulary is already familiar; the passage adds depth, structure, and academic language. Reading feels natural, not foreign.

05

Answer questions

TOEFL-style questions follow: main idea, supporting detail, vocabulary-in-context, and inference. Students apply what they've experienced, not what they've drilled.

06

Speak or write

Students respond orally or in writing — applying everything from the lesson in one integrated task. The topic they watched is now the topic they express themselves on.

What Makes Us Different

Four pillars of our method

Each pillar addresses a gap in how English is typically taught. Together, they create a learning environment where language is acquired, not crammed.

🎬

Visual becomes curriculum

Every lesson begins with a visual — a documentary, TV series, film segment, debate, or news report. Students encounter language naturally through context. Vocabulary is never memorised in isolation; it is experienced, heard, and felt first. What students watch becomes what they read, discuss, write about, and answer questions on — anchored to the same topic, vocabulary, and ideas throughout. A video on bees or vampires becomes a reading passage, a listening lecture, and a set of TOEFL-style questions.

📖

Guided reading programme

Reading is the backbone of all language learning. A dedicated weekly 1-hour guided reading session brings students together to read for pleasure — discussing ideas, sharing reactions, and discovering new words in context. Teachers lead the session so even students whose parents are too busy to read with them at home never miss out. From VCV phonics rules at Primary level to complex academic texts at iBT, every reading skill is built progressively and purposefully.

🎭

Drama, plays & performance

Students at every level perform. Younger learners act out scripts and role-play real-life scenarios — a visit to the hospital, a trip to the market. Older students debate, present, and narrate. Performance removes the fear of speaking, builds fluency under pressure, and embeds language in memory through experience. At higher levels, topics expand to match academic demands; younger learners explore language through real-world settings where words are always connected to a world they can picture.

🤖

AI as a self-tutor

At ITP and iBT level, students are taught to use AI not just as a checking tool but as a personal tutor. They learn to submit their own writing for AI critique, identify recurring patterns in their mistakes, request targeted practice on weak areas, and act on feedback independently — without waiting for the next class. This builds one of the most valuable habits in modern education: the ability to learn on your own terms, at your own pace, with the discipline to keep improving. It is not a shortcut. It is a skill.

Our Commitment

Above and beyond the standard

These are not promises made in a brochure. They are built into the curriculum, the timetable, and the way teachers work with every student.

Monthly mock tests — all five skills

Every student sits a full monthly mock test covering all five skill areas: Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing, and Language Form — regardless of level. This is not standard ETS practice; it is our commitment. Tests keep students accountable, reveal exactly where help is needed, and give parents a clear, consistent picture of their child's progress each month.

Tailored support — every learner

Where a student has specific gaps, teachers design individual learning pathways for independent practice. We understand that not every challenge can be solved in a group setting — and we do not pretend otherwise. Personalised attention is not an exception here; it is part of how we work.

Grammar with purpose

Grammar is never taught as a list of abstract rules. From parts of speech, tenses, and sentence structure at Primary level to conditionals, passive voice, and academic written expression at ITP and iBT level — every grammatical concept is introduced through texts and visuals students have already engaged with, making it intuitive rather than mechanical.

Our commitment: every skill area is taught and tested at every level, including skills ETS marks as optional. Our monthly mock tests cover all five domains across all levels. No skill is skipped; no student is left guessing.

How It Works

The teacher, the parent, the student

Three stakeholders. Each with a defined role. Each supported by the institution to do what they do best. Progress happens when all three are aligned.

🏫

The institution

Provides the structure, resources, curriculum, and environment — and continues to refine the formula based on real student outcomes and data. Monthly assessments create accountability at every level.

👩‍🏫

The teacher

Guides with method, adapts with insight, and supports with genuine care. Teachers here build relationships and tailor their approach to the learner in front of them. No two students are treated the same.

👨‍👩‍👧

The parent

Kept informed through monthly assessments and open communication. Our guided reading programme bridges the gap for parents who are too busy to read with their children at home — so no child is disadvantaged.

🎒

The student

Challenged to go further, practise independently, explore alternatives, and bring new knowledge back to class. Confidence is built one challenge at a time. Students leave knowing how to learn, not just what they were taught.
